Citizen Preparedness

Safety Guidelines & First Aid

Critical, actionable safety protocols for individuals facing emergency hazards before responder units arrive.

What to Do
  • Crawl low under smoke where air is cleaner.
  • Feel door handles with the back of your hand before opening.
  • Use stairs immediately; never use elevators during a fire.
  • Remember PASS for extinguishers: Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep.
What NOT to Do
  • Never re-enter a burning building for belongings.
  • Never pour water on electrical or grease/oil fires.
  • Do not open doors that feel hot to the touch.
Preparedness & Calling
  • Install smoke detectors on every floor.
  • Keep fire extinguisher certified and accessible.
  • Call 101 immediately once outside safely.

What to Do
  • Check responsiveness and breathing; call 108.
  • For CPR: Push hard and fast in center of chest (100–120 bpm).
  • For severe bleeding: Apply direct continuous pressure with sterile cloth.
  • For choking: Perform abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver).
What NOT to Do
  • Never give food or liquids to an unconscious person.
  • Do not remove impaled objects; stabilize them in place.
  • Do not stop CPR until medical paramedics relieve you.
Preparedness & Calling
  • Keep a certified First Aid kit in home and vehicle.
  • Know patient allergies and existing medications.
  • Call 108 or 112 immediately.

What to Do
  • Turn on hazard warning lights and place reflective triangles.
  • Turn off vehicle ignition to eliminate spark/fire hazards.
  • Check for injuries and maintain a safe barrier from oncoming traffic.
  • Under Good Samaritan laws, helpers are legally protected.
What NOT to Do
  • Do not move victims with suspected spinal injury unless car is on fire.
  • Do not remove helmets of unconscious motorcycle riders.
  • Never stand between stopped vehicles on active highways.
Preparedness & Calling
  • Always wear seatbelts and DOT/ISI approved helmets.
  • Call 108 (Medical) and 100 (Police) simultaneously.

What to Do
  • Cut main electrical supply at breaker immediately if safe.
  • Keep at least 35 feet away from downed power lines.
  • Use dry non-conductive wooden or plastic rods if emergency separation needed.
What NOT to Do
  • Never touch a victim who is still in contact with electrical current.
  • Do not touch water or puddles near downed power lines.
Preparedness & Calling
  • Call power grid emergency line 1912.
  • Check for burns and call 108 for emergency medical help.

What to Do
  • Move immediately to higher ground or upper floor.
  • Turn off main power breaker and gas valves before evacuating.
  • Drink only boiled or bottled water to prevent waterborne disease.
What NOT to Do
  • Never drive through flooded streets (Turn Around, Don't Drown).
  • Avoid walking in moving floodwaters; 6 inches can sweep you off feet.
Preparedness & Calling
  • Keep waterproof emergency go-bag with documents and medicines.
  • Contact Disaster Management at 1070.

What to Do
  • During tremors: Drop, Cover, and Hold On under sturdy table.
  • Protect head and neck with your arms.
  • If outside, move away from buildings, streetlights, and utility wires.
What NOT to Do
  • Do not run outside while shaking is occurring.
  • Never stand near glass windows, mirrors, or unanchored tall furniture.
Preparedness & Calling
  • Anchor heavy appliances to wall studs.
  • Follow official NDMA / NDRF emergency broadcast advisories.

What to Do
  • Trust your instincts; head toward populated, well-lit commercial areas.
  • Share live GPS tracking with trusted emergency contacts.
  • Use audible personal alarms or loud shouts to draw public attention.
What NOT to Do
  • Do not accept rides or food from unverified strangers.
  • Avoid using earphones with noise cancellation in isolated routes.
Preparedness & Calling
  • Women Helpline: 1091 | Child Helpline: 1098.
  • National Police Hotline: 112 / 100.

Project Architecture

About Smart Emergency Response

Smart Emergency Response (SER) is a state-of-the-art emergency incident reporting, tracking, and responder coordination prototype created to showcase how modern web standards enable frictionless civic safety interfaces.

Core Architecture & Capabilities
  • 100% Frontend Static Architecture: Completely free of PHP, MySQL, or backend servers — easily deployed to GitHub Pages.
  • Smart Priority Engine: Client-side algorithmic risk assessment weighting immediate danger, affected headcounts, and severity levels.
  • Geospatial Integration: Leaflet.js + OpenStreetMap with GPS geolocation and custom station layer markers.
  • Reactive Analytics: Chart.js telemetry dynamically refreshing upon every incident submission or status shift.
Notice: This website is an educational demonstration prototype. It does not dispatch real-world armed or medical units. During actual emergencies, always call 112.
